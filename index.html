Section 1

Sharpening and Boundary Detection

An edge is a boundary between two regions with different pixel intensities. Sharpening emphasizes these sudden changes so that objects stand out from their background. Unlike smoothing, which softens an image, sharpening makes it clearer and crisper.

Excessive sharpening introduces unwanted noise and makes the image look unnatural. Increase the strength to its maximum to observe this effect.
Section 2

Image Segmentation

Segmentation divides an image into smaller, meaningful regions to make it easier to analyze and understand.

Why segment an image?

Separate objects from the background
Identify different objects in an image
Analyze specific regions instead of the whole image
Prepare images for further processing, such as object recognition

Types of segmentation

Boundary-basedIdentifies objects by detecting their boundaries: point detection, line detection and edge detection.
Region-basedGroups pixels by similarity of intensity, texture or color: thresholding, region growing, and split and merge.

Split and merge

The image is recursively divided into quadrants until each block is uniform, then adjacent blocks with similar values are merged.

Section 3

Region Growing

Growth starts from a seed point and adds neighboring pixels that satisfy a similarity condition. It stops when no more pixels meet the criterion. Click anywhere on the grid to plant a seed.

N4 checks the top, bottom, left and right neighbors. N8 also checks the four diagonals. With a large tolerance, the region leaks into neighboring objects.
Section 4

Feature Extraction

Feature extraction detects and describes distinctive elements of an image (corners, edges, textures, shapes and patterns) and turns raw pixels into compact, informative representations.

KeypointsIndicate where interesting features are located: corners, intersections and blobs.
DescriptorsNumerical representations of the region around each keypoint, describing what it looks like.
Example, recognizing a person. Keypoint: the location of the eyes, nose and mouth. Descriptor: the shape and surrounding appearance at those locations.

Interactive demo: keypoints and descriptors

Process

1. Keypoint detectionIdentify interesting locations in the image.
2. Descriptor computationConvert the local region into numerical values.
3. Feature matchingCompare descriptors from different images.

Common methods

SIFTScale and rotation invariant. Used for object recognition and 3D modeling.
SURFA faster alternative to SIFT, robust and efficient.
ORBFAST detector with BRIEF descriptors. Suitable for real-time use.
BRISK and AKAZEEfficient binary descriptors, robust to scale and rotation.
HOG, FAST, FREAKShape descriptor, corner detector and retina-inspired descriptor.
Section 5

Feature Matching and the Brute-Force Matcher

Extraction answers "What features does this image have?" Matching answers "Which features correspond to those in another image?" The Brute-Force Matcher compares every descriptor of the first image with every descriptor of the second and selects the closest one.

Feature extractionFeature matching
Identifies important features in an imageFinds corresponding features between images
Detects keypoints and computes descriptorsCompares descriptors and calculates distances
Small distance means the descriptors are more similar.
Large distance means the descriptors are less similar.
Euclidean distance for float descriptors (SIFT, SURF). Hamming distance for binary descriptors (ORB).
